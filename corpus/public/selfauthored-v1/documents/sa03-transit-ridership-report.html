RESEARCH & PLANNING · DATA REPORT 2/4

Quarterly Ridership Data Report: second quarter

This report summarises boardings recorded by the ticket validators on all bus and tram lines of the Harrowfield network between the first of April and the thirtieth of June. It is published four times a year so that councillors, passenger groups and the operators can follow demand between the annual network reviews. Figures are counts of validations; a passenger who changes vehicle is counted once per vehicle boarded.

Demand grew on every line except the Night Owl service, which lost riders after its timetable was cut back in May. The Riverside line remains the busiest by a wide margin and now carries more than a fifth of all boardings. The share of boardings made with the monthly pass rose to 38 percent, continuing the trend that began when the pass price was frozen last autumn.

The route table in section 2 lists every route with its monthly boardings and the change between April and June. Section 3, printed in landscape, gives the twenty busiest stops by hour band. Section 4 explains how the counts are produced and where they are known to be incomplete.

4 How the counts are made

Every vehicle carries two validators, one at each door. A validation is recorded when a card or ticket is presented, whether or not a fare is charged, so transfers within the hour are counted. Validators store their records locally and upload them at the depot each night. A vehicle that misses its upload for more than three nights has its records flagged, and the affected days are estimated from the same weekday in the previous and following weeks.

In this quarter, 2.3 percent of vehicle-days were estimated in that way, most of them on the Airfield line during the replacement of its depot router. Paper tickets sold on board by the driver are not validated and are added from the ticket machine totals; they account for about one boarding in forty.
