Annual Report and Accounts for the year ended thirty-first July

From the chair

This has been the society’s busiest year since the reading room reopened. Loans rose for the third year running, the local history collection finally has a catalogue that can be searched from home, and the Thursday reading group outgrew the back room and moved into the main hall. None of it would have been possible without the forty-odd volunteers who staff the desk, mend the books and, on one memorable Saturday in February, bailed out the basement after the gutter gave way.

Our finances are sound but not comfortable. Subscriptions still provide almost half of our income, and the committee has decided to hold them at last year’s level rather than raise them, in the hope that more households will join. The grant from the county heritage fund, which paid for the cataloguing project, ends next spring, and replacing it is the main task for the coming year.

Treasurer’s notes

The accounts show a small surplus for the year, almost all of it from the sale of duplicate local history volumes to the county archive. Without that one-off sale the society would have run a deficit of a few hundred pounds, mostly because of the basement repairs. The reserve remains above the six months of running costs that the rules require.

Next year’s budget assumes flat subscriptions, a modest rise in room hire and no heritage grant from the spring onwards. The committee will report on its fundraising plans at the general meeting.
