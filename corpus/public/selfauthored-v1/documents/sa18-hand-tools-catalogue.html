Tools of Patience

Hand instruments from the Wendlecote Collection · exhibition catalogue

Introduction

The Wendlecote Collection holds some four hundred woodworking tools given by the families of craftsmen who worked in the valley and on the estuary. Most were used for decades, some for more than a century, and nearly all of them carry the marks of that use: handles worn to the shape of one hand, irons ground back to a stub, repairs made with whatever was on the bench. This exhibition shows five of them.

The objects were chosen not because they are rare but because they are typical. Each one stands for a trade that was once common here and is now almost gone, and each has a story that the donors were able to tell us. The drawings were made for the catalogue by the collection’s volunteers; photographs of every object are available in the online catalogue.

Catalogue

1 Block plane with rosewood knob

Cast iron and rosewood, maker unknown, probably late nineteenth century. Length 16 cm.

The block plane is the smallest of the bench planes and the one most often held in one hand. This example has been resharpened so many times that the iron is barely half its original length, and the knob is worn smooth on one side by a right thumb. It came to the collection from a boatbuilder’s workshop on the estuary, where it was used to fair the edges of planks before caulking.

2 Marking gauge in boxwood

Boxwood with brass wear plates, stamped with a retailer’s mark. Length 22 cm.

A marking gauge scribes a line parallel to an edge. The brass plates set into the fence show that its owner used it constantly: they were added later, when the boxwood itself had worn. The stem carries a second pin on the opposite face, filed from a nail, which turned the tool into a mortise gauge without the cost of buying one.

3 Drawknife with folding guards

Forged steel, ash handles, folding sheet-steel edge guards. Width 38 cm.

Drawknives are pulled towards the body and remove wood faster than any plane. The folding guards on this one are unusual: they swing over the edge when the knife is carried and fold back against the handles in use. The handles are replacements, turned from ash by the donor’s father when the originals split.

4 Shell auger, set of three

Forged steel, oak crossbars. Lengths 42, 46 and 51 cm.

Shell augers bore large holes in green timber for pegs and wheel hubs. The three in this set were forged by the same smith and share a crossbar pattern that is found on tools from the valley villages and nowhere else in the collection.

5 Pair of spokeshaves

Beech bodies with steel blades, one flat and one round-bottomed. Length 28 cm each.

A spokeshave is a short plane with handles on either side, used for curved work such as chair legs and wheel spokes. The pair was bought together and kept together, and the round-bottomed one still has its original blade.
